Unit 28 — Car trouble

Your car makes a strange noise. You take it to a mechanic.

From the book Speak Real English — A2–B1 English for Real Life. The book is complete on paper alone; this page is a free helper.

1. Listen to the dialogue


Slow first, then natural speed. Listen twice before you read along.

Omar: Hello. There is something wrong with my car.

Sara: Hello. What is the problem?

Omar: It makes a strange noise when I drive fast. And the brakes are not good.

Sara: I see. Can you leave the car here today?

Omar: Yes, I can. How long will it take to fix it?

Sara: It will take two days. I need to order a new part.

Omar: Two days? That is a long time. How much will it cost?

Sara: About two hundred pounds. I will call you when it is ready.

Omar: OK. Thank you. Please call me in the afternoon.

Sara: Of course. Do not worry - we will fix it.

2. Say it three ways

“My car does not work.”
Formal: There is something wrong with my car.
Neutral: My car is not working properly.
Casual: My car's playing up.

“How long will it take?”
Formal: Could you tell me how long it will take, please?
Neutral: How long will it take to fix it?
Casual: How long, then?

“How much will it cost?”
Formal: Could you tell me how much it will cost, please?
Neutral: How much will it cost?
Casual: What's the damage?

3. Words to know

Hear every word, its meaning and an example. Tap “Hear it” for one word.

mechanic — a person who fixes cars
I took my car to a mechanic.

noise — a sound you do not want
It makes a strange noise.

brakes — the parts that make a car stop
The brakes are not good.

fix — make something work again
How long will it take to fix it?

part — one piece of a car
I need to order a new part.

strange — not normal, unusual
It makes a strange noise.

ready — finished and you can use it
I will call you when it is ready.

leave — let something stay in a place
Can you leave the car here today?
