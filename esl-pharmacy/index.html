Unit 5 — At the pharmacy

You take a prescription to the pharmacy and ask for advice.

From the book Speak Real English — A2–B1 English for Real Life. The book is complete on paper alone; this page is a free helper.

1. Listen to the dialogue


Slow first, then natural speed. Listen twice before you read along.

Lena: Hello. I have a prescription from my doctor.

Sam: Hello. Let me see. Yes, this is for a cough. Do you need anything else?

Lena: Yes. Do you have any vitamins? I feel tired all the time.

Sam: Yes, we have some vitamins here. These are good for adults.

Lena: How much are they?

Sam: They are eight pounds. How many tablets do you take a day?

Lena: The doctor said two tablets, morning and evening. Is that right?

Sam: Yes. Take two tablets a day, with food. Do not take them on an empty stomach.

Lena: Thank you. Could I have some tissues too, please?

Sam: Of course. That is eleven pounds in total. Here is your change.

2. Say it three ways

“I need this medicine.”
Formal: Could I have this medicine, please?
Neutral: I need this medicine, please.
Casual: Can I grab this medicine?

“How much is it?”
Formal: Could you tell me the price, please?
Neutral: How much is it?
Casual: How much is that?

“Is this safe for me?”
Formal: Could you tell me if this is safe for me, please?
Neutral: Is this safe for me?
Casual: Is this OK for me?

3. Words to know

Hear every word, its meaning and an example. Tap “Hear it” for one word.

prescription — a paper from a doctor that says what medicine you need
I have a prescription from my doctor.

tablet — a small round piece of medicine you swallow
Take two tablets a day.

vitamins — things in food or tablets that help your body stay healthy
I take vitamins in winter.

dose — the amount of medicine you take at one time
The dose is two tablets.

side effect — a bad feeling that medicine can cause
This medicine has no side effects.

chemist — a shop where you buy medicine (also called a pharmacy)
The chemist is next to the doctor.

tissues — soft paper for your nose
Could I have some tissues, please?

change — the money you get back after you pay
Here is your change.
