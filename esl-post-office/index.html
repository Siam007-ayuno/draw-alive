Unit 26 — Sending a parcel

You send a parcel to your family. You ask about the price and the time.

From the book Speak Real English — A2–B1 English for Real Life. The book is complete on paper alone; this page is a free helper.

1. Listen to the dialogue


Slow first, then natural speed. Listen twice before you read along.

Nadia: Hello. I would like to send this parcel to Dhaka, please.

Paul: Hello. How much does it weigh? Let me check. It is two kilos.

Nadia: How much is it to send it by air?

Paul: By air it is fifteen pounds. By sea it is eight pounds, but it is slower.

Nadia: How long does it take by air?

Paul: By air it will arrive in five days. By sea it will take four weeks.

Nadia: I will send it by air, please. Can I pay by card?

Paul: Yes, you can. That is fifteen pounds. Please write the address here.

Nadia: Here it is. Can I have a receipt, please?

Paul: Of course. Here is your receipt. Have a nice day!

2. Say it three ways

“I want to send this parcel.”
Formal: I would like to send this parcel, please.
Neutral: I want to send this parcel, please.
Casual: Can I send this parcel?

“How much is it?”
Formal: Could you tell me how much it is, please?
Neutral: How much is it to send this?
Casual: How much is that?

“Can I pay by card?”
Formal: Would it be possible to pay by card, please?
Neutral: Can I pay by card, please?
Casual: Card OK?

3. Words to know

Hear every word, its meaning and an example. Tap “Hear it” for one word.

parcel — a box with something inside, sent by post
I would like to send this parcel.

weigh — how heavy something is
How much does it weigh?

kilo — a unit for weight (one thousand grams)
It is two kilos.

by air — sent in a plane, fast
By air it will arrive in five days.

by sea — sent in a ship, slow and cheap
By sea it is eight pounds.

receipt — the paper that shows you paid for something
Can I have a receipt, please?

address — the place where a person lives
Please write the address here.

stamp — a small paper you put on a letter to pay for sending it
I need two stamps, please.
