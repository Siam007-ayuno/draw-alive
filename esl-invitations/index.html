Unit 21 — Inviting someone - and saying yes or no

You invite a friend to dinner. Your friend says yes, and another friend says no politely.

From the book Speak Real English — A2–B1 English for Real Life. The book is complete on paper alone; this page is a free helper.

1. Listen to the dialogue


Slow first, then natural speed. Listen twice before you read along.

Lucy: Hi, Ben! Would you like to come to dinner on Friday?

Ben: I would love to. Thank you for inviting me. What time?

Lucy: At seven. We are going to cook pasta. Do you like pasta?

Ben: Yes, I love it. Can I bring anything?

Lucy: No, thank you. Just bring yourself. Oh, and can you ask Sara?

Ben: Of course. Sara, Lucy is asking - would you like to come to dinner on Friday?

Lucy: Sara, it is at seven, at my house.

Sara: I am afraid I can't. I have to work on Friday evening.

Lucy: That is a pity. Maybe next time?

Sara: Yes, next time. Have a lovely evening!

2. Say it three ways

“Would you like to come to dinner?”
Formal: Would you like to join us for dinner on Friday?
Neutral: Would you like to come to dinner on Friday?
Casual: Fancy dinner on Friday?

“Yes, I would love to.”
Formal: I would love to. Thank you very much for inviting me.
Neutral: I would love to. Thank you.
Casual: Yes! Love to!

“No, I cannot come.”
Formal: I am afraid I cannot make it this time.
Neutral: I am afraid I can't. I have to work.
Casual: Sorry, I can't make it.

3. Words to know

Hear every word, its meaning and an example. Tap “Hear it” for one word.

invite — ask someone to come to something
Thank you for inviting me.

dinner — the main meal in the evening
Would you like to come to dinner?

bring — take something with you to a place
Can I bring anything?

pity — a sad thing (used politely)
That is a pity.

lovely — very nice
Have a lovely evening!

next time — on another day in the future
Maybe next time?

cook — make food
We are going to cook pasta.

pasta — Italian food made from flour, like spaghetti
Do you like pasta?
