Unit 18 — Renting a flat

You want to rent a flat. You talk to the landlord about the rules.

From the book Speak Real English — A2–B1 English for Real Life. The book is complete on paper alone; this page is a free helper.

1. Listen to the dialogue


Slow first, then natural speed. Listen twice before you read along.

Sam: Good morning. I am interested in the flat. Is it still free?

Mrs Ali: Good morning. Yes, it is. The rent is six hundred pounds a month.

Sam: That is fine. What are the rules?

Mrs Ali: You must pay the rent on the first of the month. And you have to tell me if something breaks.

Sam: Of course. Is it OK to have friends over at the weekend?

Mrs Ali: Yes, that is fine. But you must not smoke inside the flat.

Sam: I do not smoke. Can I keep a cat?

Mrs Ali: I am sorry, you can't keep a pet here. The building does not allow it.

Sam: I understand. When can I move in?

Mrs Ali: You can move in on the first of next month. Here is the key.

2. Say it three ways

“Is the flat still free?”
Formal: Could you tell me if the flat is still available, please?
Neutral: Is the flat still free?
Casual: Is the flat still going?

“Can I keep a cat?”
Formal: Would it be possible to keep a cat, please?
Neutral: Can I keep a cat?
Casual: Can I have a cat here?

“When can I move in?”
Formal: When would it be possible to move in?
Neutral: When can I move in?
Casual: When can I move in, then?

3. Words to know

Hear every word, its meaning and an example. Tap “Hear it” for one word.

landlord — a person who owns a flat and lets other people live in it
I talked to the landlord about the flat.

rent — the money you pay every month to live in a flat
The rent is six hundred pounds a month.

flat — a set of rooms to live in, in a building (apartment)
I am interested in the flat.

deposit — money you pay first, which you get back when you leave
You must pay a deposit of one month's rent.

move in — start living in a new home
You can move in on the first of next month.

key — a small metal thing that opens a door
Here is the key.

allow — say that something is OK to do
The building does not allow pets.

break — stop working or become damaged
Tell me if something breaks.
