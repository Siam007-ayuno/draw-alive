Unit 4 — At the doctor

You feel ill. You see a doctor and explain the problem.

From the book Speak Real English — A2–B1 English for Real Life. The book is complete on paper alone; this page is a free helper.

1. Listen to the dialogue


Slow first, then natural speed. Listen twice before you read along.

Omar: Good morning, Doctor. I do not feel well.

Dr Lee: Good morning, Omar. What is the problem?

Omar: I have a headache and a sore throat. I feel tired all day.

Dr Lee: How long have you felt like this?

Omar: For two days. I also have a cough at night.

Dr Lee: Do you have a fever?

Omar: Yes, I think so. I feel hot and cold.

Dr Lee: Let me check your temperature. Yes, you have a fever. It is 38 degrees.

Omar: Is it serious, Doctor?

Dr Lee: No, it is not serious. Rest at home, drink water, and take this medicine twice a day.

2. Say it three ways

“I need to see a doctor.”
Formal: I would like to make an appointment with a doctor, please.
Neutral: I need to see a doctor.
Casual: I need to see a doc.

“I feel ill.”
Formal: I am afraid I am not feeling well.
Neutral: I do not feel well.
Casual: I feel awful.

“It hurts here.”
Formal: It hurts here, Doctor.
Neutral: It hurts here.
Casual: It really hurts here.

3. Words to know

Hear every word, its meaning and an example. Tap “Hear it” for one word.

headache — a pain in your head
I have a headache.

sore throat — pain in your throat when you swallow
I have a sore throat.

fever — a body temperature that is higher than normal
I have a fever.

cough — when air comes out of your lungs with a loud sound
I have a cough at night.

dizzy — when you feel that everything is turning round
I feel dizzy when I stand up.

medicine — something you take to feel better
Take this medicine twice a day.

temperature — how hot or cold your body is
Let me check your temperature.

rest — to stop work and relax so your body can get better
Rest at home for two days.
