Unit 23 — Talking to your child's teacher

You meet your child's teacher. You ask how your child is doing at school.

From the book Speak Real English — A2–B1 English for Real Life. The book is complete on paper alone; this page is a free helper.

1. Listen to the dialogue


Slow first, then natural speed. Listen twice before you read along.

Mr Khan: Good afternoon, Ms Reed. How is Sara doing at school?

Ms Reed: Good afternoon, Mr Khan. Sara is doing very well. She is good at reading.

Mr Khan: That is good to hear. What did she do today?

Ms Reed: She read a story and she played with her friends in the playground.

Mr Khan: Does she do her homework every day?

Ms Reed: Yes, she does. She always does her homework after lunch.

Mr Khan: Is there anything she needs to work on?

Ms Reed: Her writing is good, but she needs to write more slowly and carefully.

Mr Khan: Thank you. We will help her at home.

Ms Reed: Thank you. She is a lovely child. See you next month!

2. Say it three ways

“How is my child doing?”
Formal: Could you tell me how my daughter is doing at school, please?
Neutral: How is Sara doing at school?
Casual: How's Sara getting on?

“Thank you for your help.”
Formal: Thank you very much for your time and help.
Neutral: Thank you for your help.
Casual: Thanks for your help!

“We will help her at home.”
Formal: We will certainly support her at home.
Neutral: We will help her at home.
Casual: We'll help her at home.

3. Words to know

Hear every word, its meaning and an example. Tap “Hear it” for one word.

teacher — a person who teaches children at school
How is Sara doing at school?

homework — school work you do at home
She always does her homework after lunch.

playground — a place at school where children play
She played in the playground.

reading — looking at words and understanding them
She is good at reading.

writing — making words with a pen
Her writing is good.

carefully — with a lot of attention
She needs to write more slowly and carefully.

lovely — very nice
She is a lovely child.

help — do something for someone
We will help her at home.
