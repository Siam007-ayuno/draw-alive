Unit 19 — Taking a bus or a train

You buy a ticket and ask about the time of the next train.

From the book Speak Real English — A2–B1 English for Real Life. The book is complete on paper alone; this page is a free helper.

1. Listen to the dialogue


Slow first, then natural speed. Listen twice before you read along.

Tina: Excuse me. When does the next train to London leave?

Omar: It leaves at half past seven, from platform three.

Tina: And when does it arrive in London?

Omar: It arrives at nine o'clock. Are you visiting London?

Tina: Yes, I am. I am meeting a friend there. We are going to see a show.

Omar: That sounds nice. Do you need a return ticket?

Tina: Yes, please. I am coming back tomorrow evening.

Omar: A return ticket is twenty-five pounds. The train is very busy on Sunday.

Tina: That is fine. Here is the money. Thank you for your help.

Omar: You are welcome. Have a good trip!

2. Say it three ways

“When does the train leave?”
Formal: Could you tell me when the next train leaves, please?
Neutral: When does the next train leave?
Casual: When's the next train?

“I need a return ticket.”
Formal: Could I have a return ticket, please?
Neutral: I need a return ticket, please.
Casual: Return ticket, please.

“Have a good trip!”
Formal: I hope you have a wonderful trip.
Neutral: Have a good trip!
Casual: Have a great trip!

3. Words to know

Hear every word, its meaning and an example. Tap “Hear it” for one word.

platform — the place in a station where you get on a train
The train leaves from platform three.

ticket — a paper that lets you travel
Do you need a return ticket?

return ticket — a ticket for going there and coming back
A return ticket is twenty-five pounds.

single ticket — a ticket for one way only
A single ticket is fifteen pounds.

arrive — come to a place
It arrives at nine o'clock.

trip — a journey to a place and back
Have a good trip!

busy — full of people
The train is very busy on Sunday.

show — a play or a concert you watch
We are going to see a show.
