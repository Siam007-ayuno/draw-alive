Unit 2 — A normal day at work

You talk with a colleague about your daily routine.

From the book Speak Real English — A2–B1 English for Real Life. The book is complete on paper alone; this page is a free helper.

1. Listen to the dialogue


Slow first, then natural speed. Listen twice before you read along.

Sara: What time do you get up, Tom?

Tom: I get up at six every day. I have a shower and I eat breakfast.

Sara: Do you eat breakfast at home?

Tom: Yes, I do. I usually eat bread and eggs. What about you?

Sara: I get up at half past six. I never eat breakfast at home.

Tom: Why not? Are you in a hurry?

Sara: Yes. I take the train at seven. I buy coffee at the station.

Tom: I take the bus. It is slow, but I read a book on the bus.

Sara: That is nice. Do you work on Saturday?

Tom: No, I do not. I never work at the weekend. I rest and I see my friends.

2. Say it three ways

“What time do you start work?”
Formal: Could you tell me what time you start work, please?
Neutral: What time do you start work?
Casual: What time are you in?

“I am busy now.”
Formal: I am afraid I am busy at the moment.
Neutral: I am busy right now.
Casual: I am tied up right now.

“See you tomorrow.”
Formal: I look forward to seeing you tomorrow.
Neutral: See you tomorrow.
Casual: See you tomorrow!

3. Words to know

Hear every word, its meaning and an example. Tap “Hear it” for one word.

routine — the things you do every day in the same order
My morning routine is simple.

commute — the journey to work and back home
My commute takes forty minutes.

lunch break — the time in the middle of the day when you stop to eat
We have a lunch break at one.

overtime — extra work after your normal hours
I do overtime on Friday.

usually — most of the time, but not always
I usually eat at home.

sometimes — at some times, but not often
I sometimes work on Saturday.

never — not at any time
I never work at the weekend.

hurry — when you need to move fast because you have little time
I am in a hurry - the train leaves at seven.
